| Ad-safety (×1) | Advertiser-friendly, so it can be monetized |

**Why Juliane wins:** it's a real story that sounds impossible, it has a built-in countdown (11 days), and every stage is a new danger. Second-person "you" narration fits Bright Side's style, and the survival lessons ("follow the water") give viewers something useful to share.

## 2. Packaging

**Title options (A/B test the first two):**
1. She Fell 2 Miles Out of the Sky… and Survived 11 Days in the Jungle
2. A 17-Year-Old Fell From a Plane Into the Amazon. Here's How She Survived
3. The Girl Who Survived a 2-Mile Fall (And the Trick That Saved Her)

**Thumbnail:** a cartoon girl in a green dress, still strapped into a row of airplane seats, falling toward a sea of green treetops, lightning behind her. Text: **"2 MILES"**, with a red arrow pointing to the seat.

**Retention devices used in the script:**
- The first 5 seconds deliver the shock ("you just fell out of the sky").
- An open loop is set up in the hook ("the one trick that saved her life") and answered at 1:55.
- A setup is planted that pays off later ("Remember that. It's about to matter.").
- A new danger every 30–40s: storm → fall → alone → jungle → infection → maggots → rescue.
- A direct question to the viewer at 1:55 and at the end, which drives comments.
- A twist at the payoff (she went back to the jungle).

## 3. Script

### [0:00] HOOK
**VISUAL:** Black screen, a lightning flash, then a cartoon row of seats spinning through clouds.

You're 17 years old. It's Christmas Eve. And you just fell out of the sky. Two miles down, still strapped to your seat, spinning toward the Amazon rainforest. Everyone else on the plane is gone. You have one shoe, no glasses, a broken collarbone, and a small bag of candy. And this isn't a "what if." It really happened. Here's how Juliane Koepcke survived eleven days alone in the jungle, and the one trick that saved her life.

### [0:35] SETUP
**VISUAL:** Map pin on Lima, Peru → an airport → Juliane and her mom boarding, festive gift bags.

December 24th, 1971. Lima, Peru. Juliane and her mom board a flight to the jungle city of Pucallpa, to spend Christmas with Juliane's dad. Both her parents are biologists. They run a research station deep in the rainforest, so Juliane basically grew up there. Remember that. It's about to matter.

**VISUAL:** Storm clouds swallow the plane; bags fall; a lightning bolt hits the wing.

About half an hour into the flight, the plane flies straight into a massive thunderstorm. The cabin goes dark. Bags fall from the overhead bins. People scream. Then lightning hits the wing. Juliane's mom says, very quietly, "Now it's all over." And the plane breaks apart in mid-air.

### [1:15] THE FALL
**VISUAL:** The seat row spinning like a maple seed; the jungle below as a sea of green; cut to black.

Juliane is still buckled into her row of three seats. The row spins like a maple seed. Below her, the jungle looks like an endless green carpet. Then everything goes black. Scientists still argue about how she survived. The spinning seats may have slowed her down. The storm's strong updrafts may have helped. And the thick canopy of trees probably broke her fall. Either way, she wakes up the next morning on the jungle floor. Alive.

### [1:55] DAY 1: THE TRICK
**VISUAL:** Juliane, blurry-eyed, alone. A thought-bubble flashback of her dad pointing at a stream. Big text: "FOLLOW THE WATER".

She's in shock. She can barely see. And her mom is nowhere. So what would you do? Most people would stay put and wait. But Juliane remembers something her dad told her: if you're ever lost in the jungle, find water. Follow a stream downhill. Streams turn into rivers, and rivers lead to people.

### [2:25] THE JUNGLE
**VISUAL:** Wading through a stream; poking the water ahead with her one sandal; a snake and a stingray icon; a rescue plane above the trees.

So she walks. Sometimes she wades through the water itself, because it's safer than the forest floor. She uses her one shoe to test the ground ahead for snakes and stingrays. She hears rescue planes overhead, but the trees are so thick that no one can see her. Her only food is that bag of candy. And she won't eat anything else, because in the jungle, half of what looks tasty is poisonous.

### [3:05] THE TURN
**VISUAL:** A day counter ticking up to "DAY 8"; a close-up of a bandaged arm with a flies icon (cartoon, not gory).

But then something worse happens. A small cut on her arm gets infected. Flies lay eggs in it. By day eight, the wound is full of maggots. She has nothing to treat it with. And every day, she grows weaker.

### [3:30] DAY 9
**VISUAL:** A small boat tied by the river and a hut; a gasoline can; a flashback bubble of her dad treating a dog.

On the ninth day, she finds a boat tied up by the river, and a small shelter. Inside, there's a can of gasoline. She remembers how her dad once treated their dog's infected wound, so she pours the gasoline onto her arm. It burns like fire. But dozens of maggots crawl out. Then she lies down, and waits.

### [4:00] RESCUE
**VISUAL:** Three workers stepping out of the trees, surprised; a boat ride downriver; text "DAY 11: SAVED".

The next day, she hears voices. Three local workers step out of the forest and find a pale, injured girl sitting by their boat. At first, they think she might be a water spirit. Then they clean her wound, give her food, and take her downriver to get help. After eleven days alone, Juliane is saved. Out of 92 people on board, she was the only survivor.

### [4:35] PAYOFF + COMMENT BAIT
**VISUAL:** Older Juliane (stylised) in the rainforest with binoculars; a research-station sign; then a big question card.

And here's the most surprising part. Juliane didn't run from the jungle. She went back. She became a biologist like her parents, and she now helps run the very research station they started, protecting the rainforest that almost killed her, and saved her. So be honest: if you woke up alone in the jungle, would you have known to follow the water? Tell me in the comments.

## 4. Fact check before publishing
Confirm each point against at least two sources (BBC, her memoir *When I Fell From the Sky*, 2011, and Werner Herzog's documentary *Wings of Hope*, 2000):
- LANSA Flight 508, 24 Dec 1971, Lima → Pucallpa; lightning strike; 92 on board, 1 survivor.
- Fall of ~3 km (~2 miles) still strapped to a row of seats.
- Injuries (collarbone, arm cut, eye/concussion), one sandal, bag of sweets.
- Father's advice to follow water downhill; the gasoline-and-maggots episode.
- Found the boat and shelter around day 9; found by local workers and rescued around day 10–11.
- Her later role at the Panguana research station.
